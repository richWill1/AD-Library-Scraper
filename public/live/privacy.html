Privacy policy

How AdSnoop handles information when you research public Meta ads. AdSnoop is not affiliated with Meta.

Last updated 18 November 2026.

This policy describes the AdSnoop website at adsnoop.co.uk and its preview at preview.adsnoop.co.uk. It is written for UK GDPR and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR). It explains what the site actually does.

What we do not collect

Visitors do not create an account. The site has no sign-up form, no payment checkout, and no mailing list. We do not ask for your name, email address or Facebook login to use research. Notes, saved ads and pins stay in your browser. They are not uploaded to an AdSnoop account, because there is not one.

Cookies and analytics

Ordinary research does not set a cookie.

On adsnoop.co.uk only, and only after you choose Allow, the site loads Google Analytics 4, measurement id G-8BWL3SGLRE. The tag is configured with analytics storage allowed and with ad storage, ad user data and ad personalisation denied. Google signals and ad personalisation signals are turned off. Until you choose Allow, the analytics script is not added. Decline, or a later decline from Analytics settings in the footer, sets a disable flag and deletes cookies named _ga or starting with _ga_ on this site. The choice itself is the local storage item above, not a cookie. The preview hostname does not load this tag.

Google sets the _ga cookies and chooses their lifetime. This website does not set that lifetime, and it does not set how long Google keeps Analytics events. Google’s description of this data is at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

The owner reconnect page can set one first-party cookie, adlibrary-meta-login. It is HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, limited to the path /api/meta, and kept for about ten minutes. It holds an encrypted login state so the owner’s Meta redirect can be checked. It is not set when you search ads. Visitors are not asked to use that page.

Requests we see on the server

When you search, your browser calls our server with the advertiser name, website, Facebook Page link or Page id you typed, plus the coverage you selected (United Kingdom, or UK and EU). We use that only to run the search you asked for.

  • Rate limiting keeps a short count against the IP address our host passes with the request, or one shared count when that address is absent. Search, media, advertiser lookup and the owner reconnect each have their own in-memory counter. Counts expire within about a minute, or about ten minutes for reconnect attempts, and they are discarded when the server restarts. We do not store that IP address in our database.
  • Ad results are cached in memory for about five minutes. Advertiser lookup results are cached in memory for up to 24 hours, keyed by the search text, not by your identity. A news-feed cache lasts about an hour. These caches are not a visitor profile and they are lost on restart.
  • Application logs record operational events such as creative storage and collector failures. Those events include a numeric ad id, a status code and timings. They are written so they do not include a Meta access token. The application does not save your search text in a database. The host may still record request URLs, which can contain a search term, in its own logs.

If you search for a website, the server may fetch that public site and read its published name and Facebook Page link, so it can suggest the matching Page. It does not store the page’s HTML. It may keep the Page id, name, public handle and category in the advertiser directory described below.

Meta Platform Data

AdSnoop uses Meta Platform Data to run this research service. The data comes from Meta’s public Ad Library and from public Page metadata. It is retrieved with the service’s own app token, server-side. Searching the site does not connect your Facebook or Instagram account.

From the Ad Library (Graph API ads archive) we request public ad records for ads reached in the United Kingdom, or in the UK and the EU countries the coverage control lists. The fields we ask for are the ad id, Page id, Page name, ad text, link title, link caption where a lookup needs it, delivery start and stop, publisher platforms, impression range, total reach by location, target locations, target ages, and media type when classifying video or image. Health checks request a single ad id and no ad copy. We show UK reach as Meta’s figure. We do not calculate spend, conversions or a verified performance score. Labels such as funnel stage and messaging theme are our own text estimates, produced by fixed rules, not by training a model on Platform Data.

Page Public Metadata is used only to identify the Facebook Page whose public ads you asked to see. Resolving a Page handle requests the Page id, name and category. Page search requests id, name, link, category and location. The directory we keep stores Page id, name, an optional public handle, an optional category, up to 100 public ad ids and a last-seen time. Link and location from Page search are not written into that directory. Page profile pictures are loaded in your browser from Meta’s graph picture address. We do not use Page metadata to contact Page admins, to message people, or to build an advertising audience.

We do not sell Meta Platform Data. We do not license it to data brokers, and we do not share it for other companies to advertise to people. We do not use it to target ads, to inform an advertising profile about you, or to make decisions about housing, employment, credit, insurance or similar eligibility. We use it to search, display and store the public advertising and Page records this service shows, including creative files needed for those previews.

Creative images and videos may be copied from Meta’s public ad snapshot into our storage and served from our media host, including media.adsnoop.co.uk when that host is in use. Storage runs only when object storage is configured. A stored creative is kept under its numeric ad id, with format, call-to-action text and the time it was stored. The application does not give those files an automatic expiry.

The advertiser directory holds up to 1,500 public Pages. When it is over that cap, the oldest entry is dropped. It is a shared index of Pages the service has already seen. It is not a list of our visitors.

An owner-only reconnect flow exists for the operator of this website. It is not a visitor login. When it runs, Facebook Login is requested with the public_profile permission only, the account is checked against the single configured owner, and an encrypted access token for the service is stored. The application database does not store that person’s name, email address or friend list. Failed attempts keep the previous token. Short-lived login-state hashes expire after about ten minutes. A separate row stores the latest connection-check status, such as whether the token still works. That database is used only when a private database connection is configured. Visitors do not have rows in it.

Who processes data for us

We use these providers to run the site. We do not send them a visitor account, because we do not have one.

  • Render hosts the website and the media collector. When a private database is in use, Render Postgres also holds the encrypted service token. Render may keep its own access and application logs, which can include an IP address and a request URL.
  • Cloudflare R2 stores creative files and the advertiser directory when that storage is configured, and serves the public media files.
  • Meta Platforms provides the Ad Library, Page metadata and graph picture responses, and Facebook Login for the owner reconnect. Meta is the source of that Platform Data. Meta’s own terms apply to the Ad Library.
  • Google receives analytics data only if you allow analytics on adsnoop.co.uk, through the tag described above.

The Meta updates page is built on our server from public RSS feeds (Meta Newsroom, Meta for Developers, Jon Loomer and Social Media Today). Those requests are made by our server. They do not include your searches or your analytics choice.

Render, Cloudflare, Meta and Google may process data outside the United Kingdom, including in the United States. Where a transfer of personal data outside the UK is not covered by an adequacy regulation, [COMPANY LEGAL NAME] will use a UK-approved safeguard, such as the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses.

Why we use personal data

  • Legitimate interests. Providing the research tool you asked for, keeping the service reliable, rate-limiting abuse, and holding the operator’s encrypted Meta token so public Ad Library search can run. The Ad Library records we store are published by Meta for transparency. We do not use them to contact the people in them.
  • Consent. Analytics cookies and the Google Analytics tag, which run only after you allow them. You can withdraw that consent at any time from Analytics settings in the footer. Withdrawal does not affect research.
  • Legal obligation. Handling a request you or a regulator make under data protection law, where that law requires it.

How long we keep it

  • Browser storage stays until you clear it, replace it, or the site drops an expired media entry or an entry over the caps above.
  • In-memory rate limits last about a minute, or about ten minutes for owner reconnect attempts, and are lost on restart.
  • In-memory search caches last about five minutes, advertiser lookup caches up to 24 hours, and the news cache about an hour.
  • The owner login cookie and login-state records last about ten minutes. The encrypted service token is kept until the operator replaces or deletes it. The connection-check status is overwritten on the next check.
  • Creative files and the advertiser directory are kept until we delete them or, for the directory, until an older entry falls out of the 1,500 Page cap. There is no automatic deletion date for a stored creative in the application.
  • Host logs are kept for the period Render applies. We do not keep a separate copy of those logs.
  • Google Analytics retention is the setting on that property. It is not set in this website’s code.

Your rights

If UK GDPR applies, you can ask us to give you a copy of your personal data, correct it, delete it, restrict it, or give it to you in a portable form where the right applies. You can object to processing based on legitimate interests. You can withdraw analytics consent as described above. We will respond within one month, or tell you if a complicated request needs up to two further months. We do not charge a fee unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

Most of what a visitor can delete is on the visitor’s own device. We do not hold a named account to open. If you email us, tell us what you want deleted and, if you are writing about a Page or an ad, include the Page id or ad id. We may need to ask for enough detail to find the data. We can refuse a request where the law allows it, and we will explain why.

Data deletion instructions

Use these steps to ask us to delete data. The same instructions are published at adsnoop.co.uk/data-deletion.

  1. On your device, remove the site data for adsnoop.co.uk, or for preview.adsnoop.co.uk if that is where you used the site. That deletes saved ads, notes, pins, history, the analytics choice and the waiting-game score. Choosing Decline under Analytics settings in the footer turns analytics off and clears the Google Analytics cookies named above.
  2. Email [CONTACT EMAIL] with the subject “Data deletion request”. Say whether you are a visitor, a Page admin, or writing on Meta’s behalf. Include a Page id or ad id if the request is about a public Page or ad. There is no account password to send. Do not send a Meta access token.
  3. We will confirm what personal data we hold about you and delete it. If the request identifies a Page or ad that is personal data about the requester, or Meta instructs us to delete Platform Data, we will delete the matching directory entry and stored creative files we can find. Public Ad Library material that is not personal data about you may remain available from Meta and may remain in the research library.
  4. We aim to complete a deletion request within 30 days and to email you when it is done, or to tell you if we need more detail or if the law requires us to keep something.
  5. Deleting data from AdSnoop does not delete it from Meta. To remove an ad or Page from Facebook or Instagram, use Meta’s own tools.

We do not sell the data while a deletion request is open, and we do not sell it at all.

Children

AdSnoop is a business research tool. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal data, email [CONTACT EMAIL] and we will delete it.
